About Kaasni Pathshala

A learning game for CBSE Class 1, that works with the internet switched off.

Why it exists

Most learning apps for young children cost nothing to install and then earn their keep from the child: an account, a connection, and a share of that child's attention sold on to somebody else. This one is paid for the ordinary way instead. You buy it once on Google Play for ₹10, and that is the end of the transaction — nothing to subscribe to, nothing to unlock, nothing to sit through. It can afford to show no advertisements because it has already been paid for.

It asks for nothing else, either. No account, no permissions, no connection. A child can use it on a phone with no SIM, on a train, in a village with no signal, on a parent's old handset kept in a drawer. It works exactly the same in all of those places, because everything it needs is already inside it.

What is in it

Eleven subjects across 130 levels and 3,526 questions, built around the CBSE Class 1 syllabus:

Maths · Science (EVS) · GK · English · Hindi · Values · Community · Computer · Drawing · Tables · Space

Tables covers the times tables of 1 to 10, which CBSE leaves to Class 2, for a child who is ready for them early. Beyond the syllabus, three more subjects were added on purpose. Values teaches kindness, honesty and courage through everyday situations rather than rules. Community teaches what a child can actually do — which bin, closing the tap, the zebra crossing, and the emergency numbers worth memorising at six. Space answers the questions children ask about the sky long before they are taught it: the Sun, the Moon, the stars, the planets, astronauts and India's own missions, with every fact checked against NASA, ISRO or ESA.

Hindi is taught in Hindi. Its questions, answers and explanations, the teacher's praise and the buttons on the island are all in Devanagari, and the teacher speaks them in Hindi.

How it teaches

  • Everything is read aloud. A Class 1 child often cannot read a whole question yet, so a teacher's voice reads every one. A grown-up can set how fast she talks.
  • Nobody fails. A wrong answer gets a kind word and another try, then the answer itself, explained. A wobbly drawing still counts.
  • Seven ways to answer — choose, count, match, order, tap-all, trace and type — so a lesson is not twenty multiple-choice questions in a row. Every lesson has five questions with no options at all, where the child types the answer on a big keypad.
  • No clock unless you want one. A countdown can be switched on in the grown-ups corner, from 20 to 60 seconds a question; it is off to begin with.
  • A story runs over the top. Naughty monkeys carry off a mango for every question a child gets wrong, and the child wins them back from the Monkey Tree later. Spaced repetition, as a cheeky monkey.
  • Effort for grown-ups, not marks. A progress page in the grown-ups corner shows the days, the time and the rounds a child has played, worked out on the phone. It never shows a wrong answer, because a six-year-old who hears "three wrong in Maths" learns that Maths is where they fail.

Four teachers

Miss Kaasni, Miss Usha, Miss Tara and Miss Swara. A child picks one, and she narrates the whole app — and casts her own celebration when a level is finished: frost, flowers, constellations or a rainbow wave.

They are original characters, drawn in code rather than licensed from anyone.

Their voice is generated by AI and recorded into the app ahead of time. Nothing a child says or does is ever sent anywhere to produce it — the app has no internet permission at all.

Built for the phone a child actually has

  • No permissions at all. Not internet, not storage, not anything.
  • No accounts, no ads, no in-app purchases, no tracking. One payment on Google Play, and nothing inside the app asks for money again.
  • Every graphic is painted by the app itself, so it is sharp on any screen.
  • A night mode for the end of the day, tested for readability rather than simply darkened.
