Privacy Policy — Kaasni Pathshala

Last updated: 24 September 2026

Kaasni Pathshala is a learning game for young children. This policy explains what the app does with information. It is short because the app does very little: it collects nothing, sends nothing, and needs no account.

The same policy can be read inside the app, under Grown-ups Corner, so a parent can check it without going online.

The short version

  • The app does not collect any personal data.
  • The app has no internet access. It cannot send anything anywhere, because it does not ask Android for permission to use the network at all.
  • There are no accounts, no sign-in, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Everything the app remembers stays on your child's device, in the app's own private storage, and is deleted when the app is uninstalled. There is no copy of it anywhere else, not even in your own Google account.
  • Your child's name is optional. The app works fully without it.

What is stored on the device

To remember where your child got to, the app saves the following in Android's private storage for this app. No other app can read it, and neither can we.

What Why
A first name, if one is entered So the teacher's speech bubble can greet your child by name and the grown-ups' progress page can say whose it is. None of the teacher's recordings says the name
Whether the welcome screen has been done So the app opens on the world map next time
Which class and which teacher are chosen To show the right lessons and guide
Stars, coins, points and badges, and for each level its best score and how many times it has been played To remember progress, unlock the next level and show a grown-up how many lessons each island has had
Which questions were missed and how many times each, and how many have been won back So the monkeys can offer the missed ones again in a rescue round, and for the badges
For each day the app was played, how long it was open and how many rounds were finished For the grown-ups' progress page. This daily record holds only time and rounds, never answers
The date it was last played and how many days in a row For the day streak
Settings: the teacher's voice on or off and her talking speed, taps and buzzes, magic celebrations, Astro mode, and the countdown clock on or off with its seconds To keep your preferences

The name is optional. The welcome screen has a Skip for now button, and the app then simply calls your child "friend". Nothing else about your child is ever requested: no age, no birthday, no email, no phone number, no photograph, no location, no contacts.

None of this is transmitted anywhere. It exists only on the device, and uninstalling the app removes all of it.

Android would normally do two things with an app's data on your behalf: copy it into your Google account as a backup, and carry it across when a new phone is set up from an old one. This app switches both off, so no copy of your child's name or progress is ever made outside the device. It is a real cost — progress cannot be restored after an uninstall, and it does not follow a child to a new handset — and it is accepted deliberately, because it is the only way the sentence above is true without a footnote.

What the app does not do

  • It does not collect, transmit, sell or share personal data.
  • It does not contain advertising of any kind.
  • It does not contain analytics, crash reporting or any other telemetry.
  • It does not use advertising identifiers, device identifiers, IMEI, MAC address, SIM details or any persistent identifier.
  • It does not collect location, precise or approximate.
  • It does not access the camera, microphone, photos, files or contacts.
  • It does not contain social features, chat, user-generated content or links out to other apps or websites.
  • It does not contain in-app purchases.
  • It does not profile children, track behaviour across services, or show targeted or interest-based advertising.

Buying the app

Kaasni Pathshala is sold on Google Play. Google takes the payment, under Google's own terms rather than ours. The app itself plays no part in that: it contains no payment code, it never speaks to Google Play, and it never learns who bought it, or when, or whether anyone did at all.

For each sale Google gives us an order record. It holds the order number, the date, the price paid, the buyer's country, and sometimes the city, state and postal code — the parts Google needs in order to work out tax. We never see card numbers, bank details or anything a payment could be made with. Those records are used for running the developer account, for tax, and for handling a refund if one is asked for. They are used for nothing else, and they are never combined with anything the app stores on the device, because the app stores nothing that could be matched to them.

Permissions

The app requests no Android permissions. Not internet, not storage, not anything. You can confirm this yourself on the Play Store listing under App permissions, or on the device under Settings → Apps → Kaasni Pathshala → Permissions.

The teacher's voice

Every line the teacher has to say was recorded before the app was published and is stored inside it, in English and, for the Hindi lessons, in Hindi. Those recordings are generated by AI. They were produced once, by us, from a fixed list of the app's own lines — not from anything your child says or does, and not while the app is running. The app has no internet permission, so it could not send anything to a voice service even if it wanted to.

Text to speech

Your child's name is written in the teacher's speech bubble, but none of her recordings says it.

The app also carries a stand-in, for a line whose recording is ever missing: Android's built-in text-to-speech. That is a feature of the device, not a service run by us, and it is governed by the device manufacturer's privacy policy.

The stand-in would read the words in her speech bubble, and on the world map those can include your child's name. So before the app hands a line over, it chooses a voice that the phone reports works without the network, in the line's own language: English, or Hindi for anything said in Hindi. If the phone has no such voice, the line is never handed over, and its words stay on screen to be read.

Children

This app is made for children and is intended to be used by them. Because it collects no personal data and transmits nothing, there is no data for a parent to review, correct, export or delete from any server — there is no server.

If you want to erase what is on the device, you can do either of these:

  • Open the app, go to Grown-ups Corner, and choose Reset all progress.
  • Uninstall the app.

Either one is final. Because nothing is backed up, there is no copy left to come back.

We believe this approach meets the requirements of the Google Play Families policy, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the UK and EU GDPR provisions for children, and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, by the simplest available route: not collecting anything in the first place.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes what it stores or gains any ability to send data, this policy will be updated before that version is published, and the date at the top will change.
